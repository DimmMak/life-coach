# 👤 Identity-Based Change (James Clear, Atomic Habits)

## Core principle
**You don't rise to your goals; you fall to your systems. Your systems are downstream of your identity.**

```
OUTCOME-BASED        IDENTITY-BASED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━    ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
"I want to lose      "I am the kind of
 20 pounds"           person who doesn't
                      miss workouts"

"I want to write     "I am a writer"
 a book"

"I want to be more   "I am a disciplined
 disciplined"         person"
```

The first set is goal-driven (ends after the goal). The second is identity-driven (sustains forever).

## How to use it
Every behavior in your protocol should be cast as **a vote for the identity you want.**

> Each time you write a page, you are voting for "writer."
> Each time you skip a workout, you are voting for "person who doesn't work out."

The protocol's job: **make sure most daily votes go in the right direction.**

## Identity declaration formula

```
"I am the kind of person who [SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR PATTERN], because [VALUE]."
```

Examples:
- "I am the kind of person who finishes hard problems, because giving up costs more than discomfort."
- "I am the kind of person who shows up daily, because consistency beats intensity."
- "I am the kind of person who controls my mornings, because the day follows the morning."

**The user must write this in their OWN words.** Generic identity = no buy-in.

## Use this framework when diagnosis shows:
- Performance diagnosis: identity gap is large (saying X, behaving as Y)
- CBT diagnosis: limiting label ("I'm just lazy") needs replacement
- Returning user: existing protocol has habits but no overarching narrative

## Implementation in protocol
```markdown
## Identity Declaration
"{user's own words}"

## Daily Votes
Every habit in this protocol = a vote for the above identity.
Track: at end of day, how many votes did I cast for who I want to be?
```

## Key rules from Clear
- **Two-step process:** decide who you want to be → prove it with small wins
- **Consistency builds identity** — every action either reinforces or contradicts your self-concept
- **Reverse-engineer:** look at your behavior to see what identity you're CURRENTLY voting for
- **The most powerful question:** "What would [your target identity] do right now?"

## Anti-pattern
- Identity that's too aspirational ("I am a billionaire") — needs to be a behavior, not a status
- Identity that's externally imposed (parents, society) — won't survive friction
- Setting identity once and never re-stating it — daily reminder is the mechanism

📚 Source: Clear, James. *Atomic Habits* (2018)
